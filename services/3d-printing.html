What we print

Most of our printing work falls into three buckets: parts that have to look right, parts that have to work, and parts that have to survive handling. Those three needs point to different processes — which is why we quote the process rather than just the material.

  • Presentation parts — enclosure mock-ups, product models, client-facing samples with a painted or polished finish.
  • Functional prototypes — snap fits, brackets, jigs and fixtures that need to be assembled and tested.
  • End-use components — low-volume production parts, replacement components and custom fittings.
  • Model components — scale parts for plant, architectural and exhibition models.

How we choose the process

We look at the smallest feature, the surface finish you need, the load the part will carry and how many you want. Then we quote the cheapest process that still meets the requirement — and tell you what you lose if you choose cheaper.

Typical process comparison
Process Detail Strength Surface Best for
SLA (resin) Highest Moderate Smooth, paintable Presentation, fine detail
FDM (filament) Moderate High Visible layers Function, large parts, value
SLS (nylon) High High Uniform matte Complex, durable, low-volume

Files and preparation

Send what you have. We check orientation, wall thickness, support strategy and split lines before quoting, and we flag problems early rather than printing something that will fail.

  • STL / 3MF for mesh printing
  • STEP / IGES for solid models and tight fits
  • PDF / DWG drawings when tolerances matter
  • Photos or a sample part if you need it reverse engineered
